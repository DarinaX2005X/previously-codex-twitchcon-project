# Broadcast scene

`OverlayPage({client})` is the transparent 1920×1080 OBS source. The application owns capability exchange and routing. `client` implements `OverlayClient` from `index.ts`; SSE comment heartbeats must call `onHeartbeat`. State polling is two seconds and visibility expires after six seconds without a successful state or heartbeat. Privacy/cancel/reset notifications clear the scene before revalidation. The server owns trigger deduplication and the one-active/one-pending raid queue.

`ScenePlayer({plan,timeMs,playing})` renders a captured canonical plan from an external clock. Omit `timeMs` to use its local monotonic playback clock. `ScenePreview` defaults to cropping the 1920 frame to the selected safe footprint; this is useful in a dock or landing. `SceneSummary` reflows the same plan for a narrow settled reading surface. These three components have no trigger or broadcast client. A preview cannot ACK or schedule a live scene.

Optional player props include `channel`, `title`, `resolveMedia(id)`, `reducedMotion`, `soundEnabled` (false by default), `volume`, `onEnded`, `onStatus`, `className`, and `cropToLayout`. `preparedMedia` transfers the already prepared local bundle into the source without a second download. A media resolver only receives IDs present in the plan. The player uses canonical clip in/out and capture time; no URL extraction or pretend poster is used. Narration in `plan.audio` is independent of optional synthesized effects. Video remains muted.

`renderAt(tMs,plan,layout,options)` is a pure seekable frame calculation that returns positioned objects (plates, cards, slips, tabs, the mark) with per-object motion blur measured against the previous 60 fps frame. `resolveSceneLayout(plan.layout)` chooses an edge-aligned free band before prepare ACK; `sceneClip(layout)` extends the clip envelope to the screen edge only where the band already touches the safe margin and nothing is protected there. `sceneTextFits` refuses a now statement that cannot fit two lines at 80 px or larger (`text_too_long`); a chapter title that cannot fit two lines at 64 px is left out of the scene instead of shrinking. No fitting region produces `layout_required`. `audio_blocked` requires an explicit browser/OBS audio setup action.

The scene follows `review/brief/SCENE.md` and is built from `plan.scene` (see `RecapSceneSchema`): `script.ts` turns the plan into words, `timeline.ts` allocates the beats for the plan's duration (a 10 s cut keeps the chat moment and drops other chapters; a 15 s cut gives clips more time; spare time lengthens the now hold, then the scene ends early rather than idling), and `scene.ts` performs it. The greeting plate becomes the "Previously" title and docks as a corner label; chapter cards fly in as a fanned stack; one hero card carries each chapter, opens into its clip frame (its title rides down as a paper label), folds back and swaps its words; finished tabs drop into a timeline strip; real chat lines burst out of the moment's frame into a pile while a counter counts the real number, then collapse into the lore plate; everything draws into the now card, whose corner window keeps the clip playing; the now card contracts into the P mark with the real `!previously` command and leaves. Missing material shortens the scene: no chat beat without a moment, no clip frame without media, no chapters on a welcome-only cut. The moment clip is timed so that chat bursts about 2.9 s after the action (`REACTION_LEAD_MS`). Reduced motion shows a static two-part summary (now card with the latest chapter) with a short opacity transition and no video. Older plans without `scene` still play their approved beats.

Sound (`sound.ts`) is Web Audio synthesis only, fired from `sceneSoundEvents`, which reads the same timeline as the picture: swoosh, felt thump, tape-rewind chirp, card flick, shutter latch, paper slide, granular chatter swell, ceramic taps, felt tone, two-note resolve and release. Pitch varies ±3 % by event id; at most two tonal voices plus the chatter layer; master limiter; off unless enabled. `renderSceneSfxWav(events,durationMs)` renders the identical cues offline (OfflineAudioContext, browser only) to a 16-bit WAV for video editing.

Development lab (not part of production builds): run `npx vite --host 127.0.0.1 --port 4321` and open `/src/features/overlay/lab/index.html`. Query: `plan=full|10|15|welcome|queue|nochat|nomedia|manual|nowonly|compact|ru|foxlight|foxlight-10` (`foxlight` is the real plan from `fixtures/foxlight.ts`), `bg=video|poster|stand|white|none`, `guides=1` (band and protected areas), `reduced=1`, `t=<ms>`, `capture=1` (bare 1920×1080 stage). Hooks: `await window.__ready`, `await window.__seek(ms)` (resolves after media has seeked), `window.__events`, `await window.__renderSfx()` (base64 WAV).

Functional checks live in `tests/overlay`: safe geometry, arbitrary seeks, data-driven text, missing-material shortening, cut lengths, text fitting, the sound palette, reduced motion, clip bounds, first-frame readiness and timeout, audio/video cleanup, exact start/end, stale prepare completion, monotonic time, lease expiry, immediate invalidation and completed-ID reconnect. Pixels, actual-speed motion, listening, OBS routing and real media remain separate review evidence.
