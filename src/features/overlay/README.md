# Broadcast scene

`OverlayPage({client})` is the transparent 1920×1080 OBS source. The application owns capability exchange and routing. `client` implements `OverlayClient` from `index.ts`; SSE comment heartbeats must call `onHeartbeat`. State polling is two seconds and visibility expires after six seconds without a successful state or heartbeat. Privacy/cancel/reset notifications clear the scene before revalidation. The server owns trigger deduplication and the one-active/one-pending raid queue.

`ScenePlayer({plan,timeMs,playing})` renders a captured canonical plan from an external clock. Omit `timeMs` to use its local monotonic playback clock. `ScenePreview` defaults to cropping the 1920 frame to the selected safe footprint; this is useful in a dock or landing. `SceneSummary` reflows the same plan for a narrow settled reading surface. These three components have no trigger or broadcast client. A preview cannot ACK or schedule a live scene.

Optional player props include `channel`, `title`, `resolveMedia(id)`, `reducedMotion`, `soundEnabled` (false by default), `volume`, `onEnded`, `onStatus`, `className`, and `cropToLayout`. `preparedMedia` transfers the already prepared local bundle into the source without a second download. A media resolver only receives IDs present in the plan. The player uses canonical clip in/out and capture time; no URL extraction or pretend poster is used. Narration in `plan.audio` is independent of optional synthesized effects. Video remains muted.

`renderAt(tMs,plan,layout,options)` is a pure seekable frame calculation that returns positioned objects (plates, cards, slips, notches, the mark) with per-object motion blur measured against the previous 60 fps frame. `resolveSceneLayout(plan.layout)` chooses an edge-aligned free band before prepare ACK; with the default face-cam area in the top-right corner this is a 1792×640 band, and it never touches a protected area. `sceneClip(layout)` extends the clip envelope to the screen edge only where the band already touches the safe margin and nothing is protected there. Text is never dropped for length: `fitText` shrinks within each element's allowed sizes and lines (welcome 96–150 px, chapter titles 88→64 px, now statement 112→80 px, lore 36–40 px) and then `clampWords` ends at a word boundary with an ellipsis. No fitting region produces `layout_required`. `audio_blocked` requires an explicit browser/OBS audio setup action.

The scene is a TV-style "Previously on" segment (owner direction, replacing the earlier 12 s card scene). It welcomes the raiders by name over the live stream inside the free band; the "Previously on <channel>" lockup then docks as a lower-third brand bug while the picture opens to the full frame. The published chapters play in story order as a cut of their own moment clips (with their own sound when the source allows it), each with a lower-third caption (timecode chip, title and a summary line, fitted by width and clamped at a word with "…") that comes in on the cut and leaves after a few seconds so the picture is clear. A chapter without a clip becomes a full-frame title card. The chat moment's clip gets a lime tag with the real count and a vermilion tag with the running joke and its origin time. The present ("Right now") closes the cut. The picture then contracts into the P mark with the real `!previously` command, and the overlay clears.

`src/shared/montage.ts` plans the cut for both the plan builder (which sets `plan.durationMs` to the real length, at most the chosen 30 s, 60 s or 120 s) and the scene: shorter lengths shorten clips toward 4.5 s, then leave out the oldest chapters, never the chat moment or the present. Skip: the owner's Cancel on a playing recap (dock button, now labelled "Skip recap", or Escape) sets `PlaybackRef.skipAt`; the scene goes straight to its outro and the playback ends one outro later. The OBS source shows no Skip control, because viewers cannot press it; `viewerSkip` gives a real Skip button where a viewer watches in their own browser (replay, previews). Reduced motion keeps the cut but turns every travel into a cut. Older plans without `scene` still welcome and leave.

Sound (`sound.ts`) is Web Audio synthesis only, fired from `sceneSoundEvents`, which reads the same timeline as the picture: swoosh, felt thump, tape-rewind chirp, card flick, shutter latch, paper slide, granular chatter swell, ceramic taps, felt tone, two-note resolve and release. Pitch varies ±3 % by event id; at most two tonal voices plus the chatter layer; master limiter; off unless enabled. `renderSceneSfxWav(events,durationMs)` renders the identical cues offline (OfflineAudioContext, browser only) to a 16-bit WAV for video editing.

Development lab (not part of production builds): run `npx vite --host 127.0.0.1 --port 4321` and open `/src/features/overlay/lab/index.html`. Query: `plan=full|30|120|10|welcome|queue|nochat|nomedia|manual|nowonly|compact|ru|foxlight|foxlight-10`, `skip=<ms>` (`foxlight` is the real plan from `fixtures/foxlight.ts`), `bg=video|poster|stand|white|none`, `guides=1` (band and protected areas), `reduced=1`, `t=<ms>`, `capture=1` (bare 1920×1080 stage). Hooks: `await window.__ready`, `await window.__seek(ms)` (resolves after media has seeked), `window.__events`, `await window.__renderSfx()` (base64 WAV).

Functional checks live in `tests/overlay`: safe geometry, arbitrary seeks, data-driven text, missing-material shortening, cut lengths, text fitting, the sound palette, reduced motion, clip bounds, first-frame readiness and timeout, audio/video cleanup, exact start/end, stale prepare completion, monotonic time, lease expiry, immediate invalidation and completed-ID reconnect. Pixels, actual-speed motion, listening, OBS routing and real media remain separate review evidence.
